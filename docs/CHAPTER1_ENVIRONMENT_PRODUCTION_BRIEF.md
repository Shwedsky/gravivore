# Chapter 1 Environment Production Brief — Visual Replacement Production V2

Primary visual authority: approved GRAVIVORE prototype board `Концепт GRAVIVORE: Мехи и окружение.png`.

## 1. Environment identity

Chapter 1 is a dense industrial sci-fi facility built from authored modular families, not a sparse test arena. The approved board establishes dark metal infrastructure, layered floors, framed walls, reinforced gates, reactors, pipes, tanks, columns, storage/service props and mixed orange/cyan practical lighting.

The environment must feel functional. Prop placement should imply power routing, maintenance, containment, repair and industrial use.

Visual composition target: **dense edges + readable combat center**.

## 2. Global modular rules

- 1 Unity unit = 1 meter.
- Author modules on predictable grid increments where practical: 1 m, 2 m, 4 m.
- Avoid a single universal wall/floor module reused everywhere.
- Every family needs at least 2–3 silhouette variants and surface-state variants.
- Large modules require secondary shapes: ribs, braces, recesses, pipe channels, lights, grilles, door splits or machinery mounts.
- Repetition is acceptable when rotation, adjacency and supporting props break obvious patterning.
- Unique hero pieces must be reserved for navigation landmarks, elite arena and boss arena.

## 3. Floor modules

### Visual role
Create traversable industrial ground with believable facility construction and enough surface structure to avoid empty test-floor appearance.

### Shape language
Layered plates, inset maintenance strips, grates, seams, access panels, service trenches, cable channels and occasional raised rims.

### Modularity
Recommended base set:
- 2x2 m clean panel;
- 2x2 m reinforced panel;
- 2x2 m grate/service panel;
- 4x4 m large composite floor;
- edge/corner trims;
- trench/cable-channel inserts;
- damaged variant.

### Scale
Walkable top plane must remain smooth enough for gameplay and collision simplification.

### Material roles
Graphite structure, mid-gray plates, worn metal edges, limited hazard striping, dirt in recesses.

### Variation requirements
At least 3 surface patterns and 2 wear states.

### Can repeat
Base plates, grates, seams.

### Must be unique
Major arena center motifs, reactor bases, boss focal floor treatment.

## 4. Wall modules

### Visual role
Define facility boundaries and vertical density without looking like giant rectangles.

### Shape language
Framed structural bays with recessed machinery, vertical ribs, panel layering, vents, embedded conduits and maintenance doors.

### Modularity
- 2 m and 4 m straight segments;
- inner/outer corners;
- half-height wall;
- framed opening;
- service wall with pipes;
- damaged/broken segment.

### Scale
2.5–4.5 m typical vertical height, with taller landmark structures where camera allows.

### Material roles
Dark frame + mid-tone panels + sparse hazard/service accents + integrated practical lights.

### Variation
Swap inset panel group, pipe run, top cap, light unit or damage state.

### Can repeat
Structural frame family.

### Must be unique
Arena landmark walls and boss containment architecture.

## 5. Barriers

### Visual role
Low collision boundaries, lane framing and industrial safety language.

### Shape language
Reinforced rails, waist-high armored partitions, pipe-guard frames, maintenance barriers.

### Modularity
1 m / 2 m / corner pieces.

### Scale
0.6–1.4 m tall.

### Material roles
Graphite frames, worn metal, orange/yellow hazard marks.

### Variation
Solid, grated, damaged, lit, pipe-integrated.

### Can repeat
Straight rails.

### Must be unique
Boss/elite arena ritualized containment barriers.

## 6. Gates

### Visual role
Major navigation transition and area-control landmark.

### Shape language
Thick mechanical frame, split panels or retracting segments, actuator housings, locking geometry, warning lights and floor track/threshold.

### Modularity
One reusable gate family with at least:
- standard door;
- reinforced chapter gate;
- open-frame variant;
- damaged/jammed variant.

### Scale
Wide enough for player/enemy flow; visually larger than wall bays.

### Material roles
Heavy structural graphite, mid-gray armor, orange hazard paint, localized cyan/orange status lights.

### Variation
Actuator placement, top header, status lights, lock mechanism.

### Can repeat
Side frame and panel system.

### Must be unique
Boss entrance / final containment gate.

## 7. Columns

### Visual role
Vertical rhythm, structural support and light/machinery mounting.

### Shape language
Layered structural spine, base collar, mid-body ribs, top brace, integrated service ports.

### Modularity
Short, standard and tall versions; optional wall-attached half-column.

### Scale
0.5–1.0 m footprint, 2.5–5 m height.

### Material roles
Dark frame, gray shell, small orange/cyan indicators.

### Variation
Pipe-mounted, light-mounted, damaged, reinforced.

### Can repeat
Base column body.

### Must be unique
Arena landmark pylons.

## 8. Reactors

### Visual role
Major energy landmark and composition anchor. The board prominently uses tall glowing reactor machinery.

### Shape language
Cylindrical or multi-sided core chamber, armored top/bottom housings, support frame, pipes/cables, visible energy containment.

### Modularity
Small utility reactor + hero tall reactor.

### Scale
Small: 1–1.5 m footprint / 1.5–2 m tall. Hero: 1.5–2.5 m footprint / 3–5 m tall.

### Material roles
Dark frame, mid-metal shell, warm orange energy for industrial power; selected cyan reactors for advanced/service tech.

### Variation
Energy color by system role, pipe connections, armor open/closed, maintenance state.

### Can repeat
Small reactor family.

### Must be unique
Hero reactor in visual slice and boss-area power structures.

## 9. Pipes

### Visual role
Explain industrial connectivity and break flat surfaces.

### Shape language
Thick believable runs, elbow joints, clamps, valves, insulated segments, protected vertical trunks.

### Modularity
Straight lengths, 45/90-degree elbows, T-junctions, wall mounts, floor risers, valve clusters.

### Scale
Primary runs should read from gameplay camera: roughly 0.12–0.35 m diameter.

### Material roles
Dark steel, worn mid-metal, limited painted service bands.

### Variation
Diameter, clamp pattern, insulation, heat/wear.

### Can repeat
Pipe segments and joints.

### Must be unique
Major reactor connection manifolds.

## 10. Cables

### Visual role
Secondary connectivity and maintenance detail.

### Shape language
Bundled heavy cables, protected conduits, clamp points and junction boxes.

### Modularity
Short/medium cable bundles, floor-channel inserts, wall drops, hanging maintenance loops.

### Scale
Must be thick enough to survive gameplay distance; avoid hair-thin wires as primary detail.

### Material roles
Rubberized dark coating, occasional colored service bands, metal clamps.

### Variation
Bundle count and routing.

### Can repeat
Clamp/junction components.

### Must be unique
Hero machinery hookups where silhouette matters.

## 11. Tanks

### Visual role
Storage/cooling/industrial processing and mid-height scene mass.

### Shape language
Cylinders or reinforced rounded vessels with frames, caps, valves and pipe connections.

### Modularity
Vertical tank, horizontal tank, paired tank rack.

### Scale
0.6–1.2 m diameter; 1–2.5 m length/height.

### Material roles
Mid-tone steel, dark frame, heat/corrosion wear, hazard labels.

### Variation
Cap styles, piping, paint bands, damage.

### Can repeat
Base vessels.

### Must be unique
Large boss/elite-area containment tanks if used as landmarks.

## 12. Industrial machinery

### Visual role
Fill functional spaces with believable equipment rather than random crates.

### Shape language
Motors, pumps, compressors, control blocks, exchanger housings, generator assemblies.

### Modularity
Shared base frames with different upper functional modules.

### Scale
0.8–2.5 m footprints depending machine.

### Material roles
Graphite frame, gray shells, worn service surfaces, localized warm practicals.

### Variation
At least 4 distinct silhouettes.

### Can repeat
Motors, frames, vents, control panels.

### Must be unique
Hero process machinery.

## 13. Maintenance equipment

### Visual role
Communicate that the facility is serviced and occupied by machines.

### Shape language
Tool carts, service stands, diagnostic consoles, hose reels, spare-part racks, lift frames.

### Modularity
Small prop family.

### Scale
Human-adjacent industrial scale is acceptable even without humans: 0.6–1.8 m.

### Material roles
Gray metal, dark rubber, hazard paint, small service LEDs.

### Variation
Open/closed, stocked/empty, damaged.

### Can repeat
Carts, consoles, racks.

### Must be unique
None unless used as narrative landmark.

## 14. Repair Hub equipment

### Visual role
Clearly communicate safety/service/repair compared with hostile industrial space.

### Shape language
Docking arms, diagnostic pylons, charging cradle, tool manipulators, cyan energy column, maintenance consoles.

### Modularity
Build one coherent mini-kit rather than generic props recolored blue.

### Scale
Sized around G-0 footprint.

### Material roles
Cleaner graphite/gray structure, cyan/blue service emission, reduced hostile orange.

### Variation
Active/inactive states.

### Can repeat
Service lights, pylons, tool arms.

### Must be unique
Main repair hub cradle / central device.

## 15. Debris

### Visual role
Localized combat history and facility wear.

### Shape language
Broken panel fragments, damaged pipe pieces, snapped brackets, collapsed maintenance parts.

### Modularity
Small scatter kit, but placement must be authored.

### Scale
0.2–1.2 m pieces.

### Material roles
Existing environment materials with exposed raw edges and soot.

### Variation
High; obvious duplicates should be rare.

### Can repeat
Tiny fragments.

### Must be unique
Large silhouette debris used as landmark.

## 16. Scrap

### Visual role
Mechanical salvage / discarded components distinct from random rubble.

### Shape language
Recognizable machine subassemblies: rings, housings, pistons, armor plates, cable bundles.

### Modularity
Kit of 8–12 small/medium pieces.

### Scale
0.3–1.5 m.

### Material roles
Worn industrial metals, limited residue.

### Variation
Rotation/stacking plus mesh diversity.

### Can repeat
Small pieces.

### Must be unique
Large heap silhouette if used as location landmark.

## 17. Decals

### Visual role
Add authored surface history and functional signage without geometry overload.

### Families
- hazard stripes;
- maintenance numbering;
- service arrows;
- leaks/oil/grime;
- heat/soot;
- impact/scorch;
- floor wear;
- restricted-zone symbols.

### Rules
Use restrained scale and repetition. Do not cover every surface. Decals should reinforce likely function or traffic.

## 18. Emissive infrastructure

### Visual role
Navigation, system identity and focal hierarchy.

### Families
- warm orange industrial lamps;
- cyan service/repair lights;
- narrow machinery indicators;
- reactor energy chambers;
- floor-edge guidance in limited areas.

### Rules
Emission must be localized. Avoid continuous neon strips along every wall/floor edge.

## 19. Elite arena elements

### Visual role
Signal increased danger without becoming a separate art style.

### Required unique elements
- reinforced arena boundary pieces;
- 2–4 heavy power pylons or magnetics-related structures;
- stronger orange energy infrastructure;
- distinct floor center motif;
- damaged/heat-stressed surrounding machinery.

### Repeatable support
Standard walls, floors, barriers, pipes and columns may continue, but unique arena elements must change composition enough to announce an encounter.

## 20. Boss arena elements

### Visual role
Frame Custodian M-0’s scale and attacks.

### Required unique elements
- oversized containment architecture;
- heavy wall pylons / braces;
- boss-scale gate or sealed access frame;
- arena floor with broad readable attack space;
- large power conduits feeding the arena;
- visual verticals placed at edges, not blocking boss/player readability.

The boss arena must feel like it was built to contain or service something much larger than ordinary enemies.

## 21. Density composition rules

For a typical gameplay camera frame:
- 30–45% of outer frame may contain medium/tall industrial complexity;
- central combat space remains visually open enough to read player/enemy motion;
- floor receives non-blocking authored detail every few meters;
- one or two dominant machinery clusters are preferable to twenty random props;
- lighting should create depth layers: foreground framing, combat midground, background machinery.

## 22. Avoid

Reject:
- empty floors with one reactor in a corner;
- random prop scattering;
- giant simple rectangular gates/walls;
- fake scenery that has no functional visual logic;
- repetitive low-detail modular boxes;
- all props sharing same height and footprint;
- identical orange light on every object;
- high-frequency clutter inside dodge lanes;
- obvious kitbash without material/scale/style normalization.

## 23. Environment acceptance

An environment slice passes only when it looks authored at the real gameplay camera and all of these are true:
- no major wall/gate reads as primitive blockout;
- dense industrial context exists beyond the immediate combat lane;
- floor has layered construction detail without harming movement readability;
- at least one hero landmark anchors the composition;
- prop placement implies facility function;
- warm/cool lighting supports combat hierarchy;
- repeated modules are not immediately obvious;
- player and enemy silhouettes remain readable against the world.
