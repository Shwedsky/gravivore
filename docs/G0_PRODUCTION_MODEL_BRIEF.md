# G-0 Production Model Brief — Visual Replacement Production V2

Primary visual authority: approved GRAVIVORE prototype board `Концепт GRAVIVORE: Мехи и окружение.png`.

## 1. Character identity

G-0 is a compact techno-organic predatory machine organized around a visible gravity core. It is not a humanoid robot, vehicle, tank, exosuit or toy. The prototype shows a low body, multiple articulated supports, forward blade/weapon forms, pale cool armor, dark internal mechanics and a cyan core that remains the strongest player focal point.

The same identity must survive Tier 0, Tier 1 and Tier 2. Evolution means added capability, armor and containment around the same organism — not swapping to a different character.

## 2. Tier 0 — mandatory starting form

### Silhouette intent

Tier 0 must read as:

- low to the ground;
- wider than it is tall;
- front-biased;
- four-point / multi-support rather than bipedal;
- compact around a dominant circular cyan core;
- lightly armored but mechanically complete;
- agile and predatory rather than heavy.

The prototype shows a compact spider/crab-like combat form with a central glowing lens/core, small angular shell plates, distinct front appendages and visible negative space around the legs/supports.

### Body proportions

Use these as visual ratios, not hard engineering dimensions:

- overall width: 1.0 reference unit;
- overall body length: ~0.85–1.05 width;
- body height excluding upper fins: ~0.45–0.60 width;
- visible core diameter: ~0.22–0.30 overall width;
- support span should extend beyond central torso width enough to create a planted, animal-like stance;
- forward weapon/mandible projection: ~0.25–0.40 body length.

### Torso

- Compact armored cradle around the gravity core.
- Upper shell should use overlapping tapered plates, not one rounded pod.
- Front should visually narrow toward weapon/mandible forms.
- Rear should carry a denser mechanical cluster, not a smooth tail.
- Keep top-facing surfaces slightly broader than side details because of camera angle.

### Core

- The cyan gravity core is the primary identity element.
- Must be visible from standard gameplay camera in neutral locomotion.
- Core should be circular/lens-like, set into a dark containment cavity with a pale armor rim.
- Brightest emission at center, restrained falloff around surrounding channels.
- Do not use a flat emissive texture pasted onto armor; core needs depth, housing and parallax.

### Head / sensor

The prototype does not show a conventional humanoid head. Treat sensing as integrated into the forward shell/core architecture.

Acceptable:
- small recessed sensor cluster;
- narrow optical slit;
- secondary tiny cyan/white status lights.

Not acceptable:
- face;
- helmet;
- humanoid neck/head stack;
- cute eye pair.

### Shoulders

Tier 0 does not need human-like shoulders. Use lateral armor sockets where front limb/weapon structures leave the torso. They should feel like reinforced root joints rather than shoulder pads.

### Arms / forearms

Interpret the forward appendages as hybrid manipulation/weapon structures.

- tapered;
- blade or claw-adjacent;
- mounted low/front;
- mechanically jointed;
- visibly asymmetric in small detail if useful, but balanced in large silhouette.

Do not make human upper-arm/forearm anatomy.

### Pelvis

No humanoid pelvis. Use a lower central chassis ring or ventral structural cradle connecting the limb roots and core containment.

### Legs / supports

- Four primary articulated supports are the preferred Tier 0 reading from the board.
- Each support should show at least two readable articulation stages plus terminal foot/claw.
- Upper joint: armored socket.
- Mid joint: exposed dark mechanical hinge/piston group.
- Lower segment: tapered toward ground contact.
- Feet: small but clearly mechanical, not hoof-like or human boots.
- Supports must create visible negative spaces from gameplay view.

### Feet

- Three-point, clawed, or compact articulated pads are acceptable.
- Ground contact must feel stable and directional.
- Avoid identical cube feet.

### Back / rear machinery

Prototype rear detail is partly ambiguous. Preserve the visible concept logic:

- dense compact mechanism behind the core;
- cooling fins, layered plates, power routing and structural spine elements are appropriate;
- no oversized backpack;
- rear silhouette should remain lower than Tier 1/2 evolution fins.

### Joints

Every moving connection visible from the camera needs a plausible articulation story:

- pivot housing;
- bearing/ring;
- piston/linkage where useful;
- armor clearance around rotation.

A joint cannot simply be two meshes intersecting.

### Armor layering

Tier 0 should expose more mechanism than later tiers.

Recommended visual split:
- ~45% outer pale armor;
- ~40% dark structural/mechanical surfaces;
- ~15% energy/sensor/service details.

Use 2–3 armor depth layers around torso and limb roots.

### Emissive zones

Primary:
- central cyan core.

Secondary:
- narrow weapon/mandible channels;
- tiny power indicators around joint roots or rear machinery.

No full edge outlining.

### Materials

- cool pale gray armor, slightly worn;
- graphite/black metallic structure;
- medium steel for joint housings;
- cyan energy glass/emissive material;
- very limited hazard/service accents.

### Damage areas

Most believable wear:
- front blade/weapon edges;
- lower limb outer edges;
- foot contact surfaces;
- leading torso plates;
- underside dirt;
- rear vents/heat zones.

Avoid random all-over scratches.

### Expected visual mass

Tier 0 should feel compact, quick and dense rather than fragile. It is not a drone with paper-thin panels. Its mass should be concentrated around the core and limb roots.

### Gameplay-camera readability

From real portrait gameplay view:

- cyan core visible;
- front direction obvious;
- four-support stance readable;
- front weapons create a clear attack axis;
- shell has enough pale surface area to separate from dark floor;
- limbs do not visually fuse into one blob.

## 3. Tier 1 — first evolution

Tier 1 must look like the same machine with increased containment and offensive capability.

Add:
- broader lateral armor around the torso;
- more pronounced upper shell layering;
- longer or heavier forward weapon/claw structures;
- additional cyan power channels near core and weapon roots;
- modest dorsal fins/spines or containment blades;
- more protected joints.

Do not change:
- core position as primary identity;
- low quadruped/multi-support posture;
- main limb root logic;
- overall front/rear orientation;
- cool player material hierarchy.

Silhouette should become broader and more aggressive, not much taller.

## 4. Tier 2 — second evolution

Tier 2 in the prototype has a much stronger angular silhouette with prominent blade/fins and a brighter, larger visual energy presence.

Add:
- larger outer armor layers;
- more dramatic forward blades/weapon housings;
- pronounced dorsal/side spines or fins;
- stronger containment geometry around the core;
- denser mechanical layering at limb roots;
- wider stance / larger perceived footprint without changing the gameplay root arbitrarily.

Tier 2 may feel more dangerous and partially overgrown by its technology, but it must still be recognizably G-0.

Avoid solving evolution by:
- simply scaling the whole body;
- adding a huge humanoid torso;
- replacing the core with another color;
- attaching unrelated decorative spikes everywhere.

## 5. Tier continuity rules

Across all tiers preserve:

- gravity core position and cyan identity;
- basic limb count and articulation family;
- low predatory posture;
- pale armor over dark mechanism;
- forward-blade language;
- same broad mechanical ancestry in feet, joints and rear machinery.

Evolution should look like progressive armor/weapon growth around a stable anatomy.

## 6. Animation-ready modeling notes

- Independent articulated limb segments.
- Clear mechanical pivots aligned to actual hinge axes.
- No fused upper/lower limb geometry if it must articulate.
- Core housing should remain independent enough for subtle pulse/containment animation.
- Weapon/blade structures should have sockets for attack VFX origins.
- Leave clearance around joints so locomotion does not clip immediately.
- Support root, core, attack sockets and effect anchors must be deterministic and named.

## 7. Modeling priority by camera importance

Highest fidelity:
1. upper torso/core;
2. front weapons/mandibles;
3. upper-facing armor plates;
4. limb root joints;
5. outer leg silhouettes;
6. rear machinery visible from camera.

Lower fidelity:
- underside hidden surfaces;
- tiny rear-facing bolts;
- invisible internal mechanics.

## 8. Do not accidentally turn G-0 into...

### Generic humanoid robot
Reject if it has human torso, shoulders, arms, pelvis and two legs arranged like a person.

### Military exosuit
Reject if it looks designed to contain a human body or wears recognizable body armor anatomy.

### Boxy mech
Reject if the core identity becomes a rectangular chassis with legs attached.

### Warhammer-like heavy armor
Reject if visual mass becomes stacked gothic/heavy plate, monumental shoulder blocks or cathedral-like ornament.

### Anime Gundam clone
Reject if it develops humanoid heroic proportions, face/helmet, V-fin language or human stance.

### Toy robot
Reject if proportions become cute, rounded, oversimplified, glossy plastic or visually lightweight.

### Tank with legs
Reject if the upper body reads primarily as a vehicle hull or turret.

## 9. Acceptance test

A Tier 0 model passes only when all are true:

- recognizable as the prototype G-0 family at gameplay camera distance;
- core remains primary focal point;
- low multi-support silhouette is clear without color;
- armor is layered, not monolithic;
- joints look articulatable;
- front attack direction is obvious;
- surface detail survives mobile scale without becoming noise;
- model does not need emissive brightness to compensate for a weak silhouette.

If any of those fail, do not proceed to Tier 1/2 production.
